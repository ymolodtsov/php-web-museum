Timeline

A rough chronology of the software represented in this museum. Dates mark first release or the start of a program's mainstream adoption, not necessarily both.

2001phpBB 2 released. Developers unhappy with PHP-Nuke's direction fork it as PostNuke.
2002PHP-Nuke reaches peak popularity as a news-portal engine. osCommerce gains traction among small online retailers.
2003XOOPS forks away from a PHP-Nuke-derived codebase. Coppermine Photo Gallery becomes the standard self-hosted gallery.
2004Mambo is at its most widely deployed. e107 gains a following among clan and hobbyist sites.
2005Core Mambo developers fork the project; Joomla 1.0 is released. vBulletin 3 and Invision Power Board 2 dominate the commercial forum market. MediaWiki's MonoBook skin becomes a familiar sight well beyond Wikipedia. MyBB 1.0 is released in December.
2006WordPress 2 ships the Kubrick default theme. Simple Machines Forum 1.1 and PunBB attract users seeking lighter alternatives to vBulletin. Vanilla 1.0 drops categories-first browsing in favour of a single list of discussions.
2007Joomla 1.5 released. phpBB 3.0 "Olympus" ships in December with the new prosilver theme. The Mambo/Joomla template style becomes a common sight for small business sites.
2008Drupal 6 released, widely adopted by universities, NGOs and technical organizations.
2009–2012WordPress absorbs much of the simple-CMS and blogging market. Forum traffic gradually migrates toward Facebook groups and, later, Reddit and Discord. Several packages in this museum see their last major release during this period.
